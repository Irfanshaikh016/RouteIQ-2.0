1. Introduction

Transportation and logistics in the North Eastern Region (NER) of India face persistent challenges from difficult terrain, heavy rainfall, landslides, floods, road damage, congestion, and limited connectivity. Conventional route-planning tools optimize purely for distance or time, which is inadequate in a region where the shortest path is frequently the most fragile one.

RouteIQ 2.0 reframes the problem as a continuously-learning, graph-based decision system: the road network is modelled as a dynamic graph whose edge weights (time, risk, cost, emissions) are updated in near real time from heterogeneous data streams, and routing decisions are produced by a combination of graph learning, sequence forecasting, and reinforcement-learning-driven optimization rather than a single static model.

2. Problem Statement & Research Gap

Existing systems (including the baseline RouteIQ prototype) treat risk prediction as an independent, per-route classification problem and treat rerouting as a manual, reactive process. This has three limitations that the advanced design addresses:

Independent risk scoring ignores network effects — a landslide on one segment changes the effective risk of neighbouring and downstream segments, which a per-route classifier cannot capture but a Graph Neural Network can.

Static models degrade over a monsoon season; they need to be updated continuously rather than retrained periodically — motivating an online/streaming learning pipeline and reinforcement learning for the routing policy itself.

Field data is sensitive and often can't be centralized across state transport offices or private fleet operators — motivating federated learning.

Manual field reporting is too slow for landslide-prone corridors — motivating automated computer-vision-based damage detection from satellite and drone imagery.

3. Proposed Advanced System

RouteIQ 2.0 models the NER transportation network as a dynamic, attributed graph G = (V, E), where nodes represent junctions/settlements and edges represent road/rail/waterway segments carrying live and historical attributes (weather, terrain, traffic, incident probability, capacity, container/shipment compatibility).

4. Advanced AI / ML Components

4.1 Graph Neural Network (GNN) for Network-Aware Risk Propagation

Instead of scoring each route independently, a Graph Attention Network (GAT) or GraphSAGE model is trained over the full road-network graph. Each node/edge embedding is learned from neighbouring segments, so a disruption automatically propagates a risk signal to nearby and downstream edges — modelling the real-world fact that a landslide can isolate an entire cluster of villages, not just one road segment.

Inputs: rainfall intensity, slope/terrain, soil saturation index, historical landslide/flood frequency, live traffic density, road-surface condition, proximity to known fault/erosion zones.

Output: a per-edge disruption probability with an associated confidence interval (via Monte-Carlo dropout or a Bayesian GNN).

4.2 Spatio-Temporal Forecasting (Transformer / LSTM)

A sequence model forecasts how weather- and traffic-driven risk will evolve over the next 6–48 hours per corridor, enabling RouteIQ to warn about a route that is currently safe but is forecast to become risky mid-journey — critical for long-haul shipments crossing the region.

4.3 Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Fleet Routing

A Deep Q-Network / Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent treats fleet-wide routing as a sequential decision problem: state = current graph risk/traffic snapshot + fleet positions + delivery priorities; action = route/segment assignment per vehicle; reward = weighted function of on-time delivery, risk exposure, fuel cost, and SLA penalties. This allows the system to learn rerouting policies that go beyond simple 'recompute shortest safe path' logic, e.g. proactively holding a shipment for two hours rather than sending it into a corridor about to flood.

4.4 Computer Vision for Automated Damage Detection

A vision model (YOLOv8 or a segmentation network such as U-Net/DeepLabV3) is trained on satellite tiles and drone imagery to automatically detect landslides, road washouts, and flooding, converting damage detections directly into graph-edge risk updates without waiting for manual field reports.

4.5 Federated Learning for Privacy-Preserving Field Data

District transport offices, private fleet operators, and government agencies can each train the risk model locally on their own field-report data and share only encrypted model updates (via Federated Averaging), preserving data sovereignty while still improving a shared global risk model.

4.6 Explainable AI (XAI) Layer

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values are computed for each risk prediction so that dispatchers see not just 'Route B: 87% risk' but a ranked breakdown, e.g. 'Rainfall (last 6h): +41%, Slope gradient: +22%, Reported incident proximity: +18%, Traffic: +6%' — improving trust and auditability of AI-driven decisions.

5. Additional Advanced Features

5.1 Digital Twin of the Road Network

A simulation environment (built on SUMO — Simulation of Urban MObility, extended with terrain/weather layers) mirrors the real road network. Planners can simulate 'what-if' scenarios — e.g. a bridge closure or a monsoon spike — and see projected fleet-wide impact before it happens, and RL policies can be safely trained/validated in the twin before deployment.

5.2 IoT & Edge Sensor Network

Low-power soil-moisture and vibration sensors on known landslide-prone slopes, transmitting via LoRaWAN.

Edge-computing gateways that pre-process sensor and camera data locally (reducing bandwidth needs in low-connectivity NER terrain) and push only risk-relevant events upstream.

5.3 Drone-Based Aerial Survey Integration

Scheduled or on-demand drone flights over high-risk corridors feed imagery directly into the computer-vision pipeline (4.4), providing a faster, cheaper alternative to full satellite tasking for localized verification.

5.4 Multi-Modal Logistics Optimization

Beyond road transport, the graph incorporates rail sidings, river/waterway transport (relevant for Brahmaputra and Barak valley corridors), and short-haul air cargo options for emergency relief, letting the optimizer recommend modal shifts (e.g., 'road to nearest railhead, then rail') when road risk is high.

5.5 Blockchain-Based Provenance (Optional Module)

A permissioned blockchain (e.g., Hyperledger Fabric) records immutable timestamps for field-incident reports and shipment custody events, useful for insurance claims, SLA disputes, and government audit trails in disaster-relief logistics.

5.6 Predictive Fleet Maintenance

Vehicle telemetry (engine health, tire pressure, past terrain exposure) feeds a separate predictive-maintenance model, flagging vehicles at higher breakdown risk before assigning them to difficult, high-risk corridors.

5.7 Carbon & Cost-Aware Routing

A tertiary optimization objective estimates fuel consumption and CO2 emissions per route/vehicle/load combination, allowing an optional 'Green Mode' alongside Fast and Safe modes.

6. Updated System Workflow

1. Continuous ingestion: weather, IoT sensors, GPS telemetry, satellite/drone imagery, crowdsourced & field reports stream into Kafka topics.

2. Stream processing updates the live road-network graph (PostGIS + in-memory graph store) with fresh edge attributes.

3. GNN + Transformer forecast per-edge current and near-future disruption probability with confidence bounds.

4. Computer-vision pipeline scans new satellite/drone tiles for undetected damage and injects new graph updates.

5. Multi-objective search (NSGA-II) generates a Pareto set of candidate routes across time, risk, cost, and emissions.

6. RL routing-policy agent selects/adjusts assignments across the whole active fleet, not just the current shipment.

7. XAI layer generates a human-readable explanation for the top recommendation.

8. Dispatcher/user reviews via GIS dashboard or mobile app; can override, and overrides feed back as training signal (human-in-the-loop learning).

9. Digital twin continuously validates policy changes in simulation before they're pushed to production routing.

10. Blockchain ledger (if enabled) timestamps confirmed incidents and shipment milestones.

8. Evaluation Metrics

Risk-prediction accuracy: AUC-ROC / Precision-Recall for disruption classification, calibration error for confidence intervals.

Routing quality: average delay reduction vs. shortest-path baseline, % of shipments avoiding high-risk segments.

RL policy performance: cumulative fleet-wide reward, SLA compliance rate, simulated-vs-real deployment gap (measured via digital twin).

Computer-vision detection: mAP (mean average precision) for damage detection, false-alarm rate.

System performance: end-to-end recommendation latency, Kafka pipeline throughput, dashboard update lag.

Federated learning: model accuracy parity between federated and centralized training, communication overhead.

9. Expected Outcome

RouteIQ 2.0 is expected to demonstrate a research-grade, production-realistic architecture for adaptive logistics in geographically challenging regions — moving beyond a single predictive model to a coordinated system of graph learning, forecasting, reinforcement-learning-driven optimization, computer vision, and simulation, while remaining deployable via standard microservice and edge-computing practices.

10. Future Scope

Integration with national disaster-management and government road databases (e.g., NDMA, PWD feeds).

Cross-border corridor risk modelling for NER trade routes into Bangladesh/Myanmar.

Fully autonomous drone-based post-disaster corridor assessment.

Federated multi-agent RL across neighbouring states' logistics networks.

On-device (edge) inference for offline risk scoring in zero-connectivity zones.

Integration with insurance and disaster-relief funding workflows via the blockchain provenance layer.

11. Conclusion

By evolving from a single-model, rule-augmented recommender into a graph-native, multi-model, self-learning system — combining GNNs, spatio-temporal forecasting, reinforcement learning, computer vision, federated learning, and digital-twin simulation — RouteIQ 2.0 elevates the original concept into a research-grade platform suitable for both academic contribution and real-world deployment in the North Eastern Region's uniquely challenging logistics environment.
